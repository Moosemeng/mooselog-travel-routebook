# Adaptive Color System

Use this reference before choosing paper, card, route, and illustration colors. Never default every journal to sand, cream, sepia, or kraft paper.

## Color Decision Order

1. Read the destination images first. If photos are unavailable, use the destination's dominant landscape, season, weather, architecture, and time of day.
2. Extract five functional colors:
   - `page-ground`: the atmospheric page field;
   - `landscape-primary`: the dominant environmental hue;
   - `landscape-secondary`: a balancing natural or architectural hue;
   - `route-accent`: a high-contrast route color;
   - `warning-accent`: a separate restrained alert color.
3. Build card fills by mixing the page ground with the landscape colors at low saturation. Do not introduce unrelated pastel colors.
4. Verify the route stays visible over every map region with a light or dark halo.
5. Re-check text contrast after the map is composited.

## Ground Rules

- The page ground may be cool, green, blue, gray, pink, white, black, or earth-toned. It does not have to resemble old paper.
- Preserve tactile paper grain, but tint the fibers to the destination atmosphere.
- Keep the left and right halves in one chromatic world. Sample left-card fills and borders from the finished map.
- Avoid one-note palettes. Use one dominant environmental family plus two supporting contrasts.
- Do not use beige merely to signal "journal" or "hand-drawn".

## Destination Examples

- Dolomites in summer: mist white, alpine green, limestone gray, lake blue; route in warm coral or ochre.
- Ejina in October: sun-bleached ivory, poplar gold, canyon red, Juyan Lake blue; route in dark brick red.
- Jiangnan in spring rain: rice-paper white, celadon, ink gray, wet-stone blue; route in cinnabar.
- Yunnan plateau: cloud white, lake turquoise, mountain green, local architecture red or indigo.
- Coastal Fujian: salt white, sea blue-green, granite gray, roof-tile red.
- Winter Northeast China: snow white, pine green, ice blue, warm lantern red used sparingly.
- Tokyo at night: cool gray, asphalt black, sign red, electric cyan used selectively.

## Missing Photos

When the user has not supplied photos, state internally that the palette is destination-derived rather than photo-derived. Prefer a reversible first draft and update the palette after photos arrive.

## Quality Check

- Could the destination still be guessed from the palette with the text hidden?
- Does the page ground support the actual environment instead of imposing a generic vintage mood?
- Are map labels, route marks, and body text readable at phone scale?
