# China Travel Visual System

Use this reference for every `domestic-china` journal. Treat domestic China as the flagship experience of the skill, not as the international template with the flag removed.

## Core Principle

Build a contemporary Chinese travel journal whose visual language grows from the destination's region, landscape, history, craft, and everyday culture. Amplify Chinese design confidently, but never flatten China into one generic antique style.

## Three-Layer Design Model

### 1. Shared Chinese Editorial Grammar

Use several of these structural devices, chosen with restraint:

- vertical title slips, folded book labels, seal-like day markers, stitched or thread-bound page edges;
- handscroll or album-leaf route rhythm instead of only Western dotted-map composition;
- ink-wash spatial transitions, mineral-color washes, rubbing textures, carved-print edges, rice-paper fibers;
- Chinese cartographic symbols for mountains, water, city gates, bridges, pavilions, villages, passes, ports, fields, and roads;
- short poetic or geographic microcopy as an accent, never as decorative filler;
- compact cinnabar seals only when they carry real meaning such as day, route, weather, or author.

Do not use all devices at once. Choose 2-4 recurring motifs and make them part of the composition rather than stickers pasted on top.

### 2. Regional Visual Dialect

Choose the visual dialect from the actual destination:

- Northwest desert and frontier: Dunhuang/mineral-color restraint, Han-slip proportions, frontier maps, wind-eroded edges, seal-script-like route tabs; colors from desert, lake, poplar, and canyon.
- Jiangnan water towns: album-leaf composition, ink and celadon, rain marks, lattice-window geometry, bridge and canal symbols, silk or xuan-paper softness.
- Beijing and northern historic cities: palace vermilion, gray brick, roof-tile rhythm, city-axis diagrams, archival map fragments; avoid turning every page imperial.
- Southwest ethnic regions: use landscape and verified local craft geometry only when culturally sourced. Do not invent or generically mix ethnic motifs.
- Tibetan plateau and western highlands: expansive sky/land proportions, mineral blue and red, terrain diagrams; do not use sacred symbols as decoration.
- Lingnan and coastal south: arcade shadows, tile and window patterns, banyan green, sea-weather color, lighter paper atmosphere.
- Northeast: woodcut energy, snow fields, birch/pine structure, railway and industrial archive cues, restrained festival red.
- Contemporary Chinese cities: transit diagrams, local signage proportions, newspaper or ticket fragments, modern editorial typography; Chinese style need not look ancient.

### 3. Destination-Specific Evidence

Require at least three visual signals unique to the trip, such as:

- a local landform or skyline;
- a regional architectural silhouette;
- a plant, food, transport mode, craft, or historic map symbol;
- a locally meaningful color relationship;
- a place-name treatment inspired by verified local material culture.

If these signals are absent, the domestic page is too generic.

## Domestic Page Architectures

Do not always reuse the same left-card/right-map frame. Select one:

- `中国行旅图册`: information leaves on the left, continuous illustrated journey map on the right; best for multi-day guides.
- `山水长卷`: the map moves vertically like a handscroll with itinerary annotations beside it; best for landscapes and road trips.
- `城市折页`: folded local-map rhythm, ticket labels, street signs, food and transit notes; best for citywalks.
- `地方志手记`: denser archival structure with local facts, route chronology, stamps, rubbings, and field notes; best for history-heavy destinations.
- `节气游记`: season, plants, weather, food, and light drive the page; best for blossom, harvest, snow, and autumn-color trips.

The classic split layout remains available, but adapt its proportions, edge treatment, title system, and cards to the chosen Chinese architecture.

## Typography

- Continue using bundled commercially usable fonts for every rendered glyph.
- Express Chinese character through composition, hierarchy, vertical setting, spacing, seals, and paper forms rather than unlicensed calligraphy fonts.
- Do not simulate calligraphy with distorted body fonts.
- Keep practical copy horizontal and highly legible. Reserve vertical text for short titles or labels.

## Cultural Safety

- Verify specific heritage, religious, ethnic, or political symbols before use.
- Do not invent minority patterns or combine motifs from unrelated regions.
- Do not use sacred scripts, prayer flags, religious icons, official emblems, maps, seals, or national symbols as casual decoration.
- Avoid costume caricatures and generic "ancient China" clipart.
- Use cultural references as design structure and place evidence, not exotic ornament.

## Domestic Quality Bar

Domestic outputs should receive more visual specificity, not less. Before delivery, ask:

- Does this look designed for this Chinese destination rather than translated from an overseas template?
- Are the Chinese elements structural and regionally appropriate?
- Can the page feel contemporary, useful, and culturally confident without becoming a costume-drama poster?
- Is the guide still phone-legible and easy to save?
